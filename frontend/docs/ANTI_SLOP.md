# Anti-slop criteria

This is a project editing rubric, not a scientific classification of AI-generated work. Shlok asked for concrete language and a minimal homepage without unnecessary eyebrows or decorative text. Apply these checks to human and generated contributions alike.

## Content checks

- Every sentence must help someone understand the product, choose a next action, or interpret a result. Delete filler that survives unchanged on an unrelated SaaS site.
- Name the actual action: “Open a case”, “Search by instrument”, “Open a video”. Avoid “unlock”, “revolutionize”, “seamless”, “intelligent insights”, and unsupported superlatives.
- No eyebrow labels that repeat the heading, strings of uppercase buzzwords, gratuitous badges, fake testimonials, fabricated metrics or partner logos.
- Describe what exists. Local uploads support playback, not indexed search. Experimental boxes are not validated anatomy. Do not imply clinical accuracy or improved patient outcomes.
- Keep one primary action per section. Links describe their destination; buttons describe what happens. A decorative control must never look interactive.
- Short copy is not automatically clear. Keep limitations next to the action they affect. Use surgical terminology where precise; avoid jargon added only to sound technical.
- Use the Surg+F / Ctrl+F idea once, then show the actual video-finding workflow. Never intercept the browser's Ctrl+F shortcut.

## Visual checks

- No default three-card feature grid, gradient blobs, glass panels or decorative dashboards unless they explain this product.
- No random icons beside every heading. Crosshair geometry is decorative, sparse and hidden from assistive technology; it is not a clinical measurement.
- Whitespace and typography provide hierarchy. Do not fill an empty area with invented content.
- Motion must respond to a meaningful action and remain optional. No scroll hijacking, cursor replacement, infinite marquee or flashing attention cue.
- Review narrow screens, keyboard focus and reduced-motion behavior. Screenshots and actual interactions are evidence; a successful build is not visual verification.

## Review questions

Can a new visitor explain what the product does and open a case? Does every visible claim match implemented behavior? Can any sentence, label, animation or panel be removed without losing meaning? Are sample/annotation/prediction sources distinguishable? Does it still work without motion?

## Research behind the rubric

[GOV.UK content design](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/understand-content-design/) grounds content in user needs and plain English. [NHS content guidance](https://digital.nhs.uk/about-nhs-digital/corporate-information-and-documents/our-style-guidelines/content-style-guide/creating-content) emphasizes meaningful headings and reducing clutter for readers. [W3C animation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) explains why nonessential motion should respect user preferences. The specific banned patterns above are our product choices, not rules claimed by these sources.
